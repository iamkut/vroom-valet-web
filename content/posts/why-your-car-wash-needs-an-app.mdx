Running a car wash without a booking system works — until it doesn't.

Walk-ins fill your bays on a quiet Tuesday. On a Saturday morning in Sandton, the same approach
means a queue of irritated customers, staff who don't know what's coming, and a bay that stays
empty because someone got fed up waiting and left.

## The real cost of no system

It's not just the lost wash. It's the customer who waited 40 minutes, got annoyed, and never came back.
It's the staff member who checked in three cars at the same time because nobody knew who was next.
It's you, the operator, spending Sunday evening going through WhatsApp messages to figure out
what tomorrow looks like.

## What a booking system actually changes

**Capacity is predictable.** You set how many cars your bays can handle per hour.
Customers book into real slots. When you're full, you're full — and customers know before they arrive.

**Staff know the day before it starts.** The morning queue view tells your team what's booked,
what time each car should arrive, and what service they're in for. No surprises at 8:15.

**Customers stop hovering.** Real-time stage tracking — received, washing, drying, ready —
means customers can go to the shops and come back when their phone buzzes.
The bay isn't blocked by someone standing next to their car.

**No-shows leave a trail.** When someone books and doesn't show, you have a record.
Over time, that tells you something useful about your slot structure, your pricing,
or your reminder setup.
